# ADR-0003: Rules make the decision; ML may only re-rank

**Status:** Accepted · 2026-10-08

## Context

All our data is synthetic, generated by our own simulator. Any model trained on it learns our
generator's hazard function, not vehicle physics. A reported AUC would measure how well the model
reverse-engineered code we wrote — a number that is simultaneously excellent and meaningless.

This failure is not hypothetical. Epic's sepsis prediction model was deployed across hundreds of
hospitals on a vendor-claimed AUC of 0.76–0.83. External validation measured 0.63: it fired on 18%
of all patients while missing 67% of actual sepsis cases.

## Options considered

1. **ML as the decision path**, with an honest caveat in the report. Rejected: the caveat does not
   change what the demo shows, and the headline claim stays unsupportable.
2. **No ML at all.** Honest, but forfeits a graded component and a genuine capability.
3. **Rules decide; ML re-ranks within what rules surfaced.** Chosen.

## Decision

**VEWS** — an additive early-warning score ported from the clinical NEWS2 score — owns the
OPEN/SUPPRESS decision. Each of 9 signals scores 0–3 from a versioned band table; the total, plus
an override for any single critical signal, determines severity.

The ML model may only reorder items the rules have already surfaced. It can never promote a
vehicle onto the board on its own.

## Consequences

- **A model that memorised our simulator cannot manufacture a false alert**, because it only
  reorders a set the rules chose. That structural property, not a disclaimer, is what makes the
  claim safe.
- Every alert is explainable down to a specific sentence ("aux battery resting voltage 11.4 V
  scored 3"), which a dispatcher can dispute. "0.87" is not disputable.
- Alerts record the band-table version that scored them, so a decision made months ago can be
  reconstructed exactly.
- We forfeit whatever recall a pure model might reach on genuinely novel signal combinations.
  Accepted deliberately: on synthetic data we could never have verified that recall anyway.
- We do not quote offline AUC as a product claim anywhere.
- The simulator reinforces the honesty: 4 of its 9 fault mechanisms are deliberately invisible in
  telemetry, so achievable recall is bounded by physics rather than by our cleverness.
